streak society

An exclusive tier for Duolingo's most dedicated learners to celebrate and encourage high streaks.

Design Lead

What we are solving

Learners were reaching huge milestones, but nothing special was happening

We wanted learners who have been using Duolingo daily for a long time to feel seen and special by automatically inducting them into an exclusive virtual club.

Summary

We see you, hardcore learners!

A "streak" is an achievement in Duolingo that happens when a user logs in and earns points (XP) for consecutive days. Using the app 5 days in a row earns the user a 5-day streak, 100 days earns a 100-day streak, and so on. The bigger the streak, the more it matters to users, as it shows consistency and dedication, and triggers loss aversion.

People love to celebrate their high streaks in the most creative ways, from sharing screenshots to fancy custom cakes. Even Duolingo staff will find ways to share accomplishments with each other internally.

Duolingo user celebrating 1000 consecutive days.

Duolingo staff member sharing a streak accomplishment.

Streaks also improve learning and significantly impact the company's monetization efforts. The way Duolingo acknowledged a high streak was, however, lackluster. The same standard screen was displayed every time, no matter the milestone.

During Duolingo's 3-day hackathon, we invited the company to imagine a feature that celebrated our high achievers' efforts with intention. Something that would motivate long-time learners to maintain their status, and others to strive to join them. Something like a secret club...

Inspiration and iteration

Hackathon, our chance to celebrate these long streaks

Those who reach 1000 consecutive days are hugely valuable for a company focused on DAUs. The more dedicated user is more likely to log in the next day compared to the average person, but is also at higher risk of burning out. We were confident we could address this and started by brainstorming:

How can we celebrate high streak folks, and make having a high streak a desirable goal for everyone? Would this have a positive impact on retention and DAUs, while not hurting other important metrics? How can we make this enticing, while still scalable and low-cost? What is the potential for expansion and interesting iterations?

We sought helpful data, such as how many people reached high milestones (like 500, 1000, 2000 consecutive days of app use), and started calculating the initiative and incentive cost to better flesh out the idea.

Visualized mood boards to spark the team's creativity

After initial discussions with the team, I summarized our direction in a simple mood board showing things that feel exclusive. An exclusive club behind a velvet rope that you can/want to brag about, and that money can't buy, but rather can only be earned with effort. We drew inspiration from airport lounges (something that money can buy but has that special club vibe we wanted) and Saturday Night Live's "Five-Timers Club," among others.

Like any proper society, we wanted our club members to receive a coveted token of membership to display. Wikipedia offers limited edition pins... do we make a pin? Should we have tiers? We went with pin and plushie.

Reinterpreted core brand elements to serve more snobbery

We chose to use the Duo plushie (a coveted item that was not available for sale) as a "carrot," which would unlock for Streak Society members as a reward in a merch store. Inductees would also be able to automatically claim a free streak flame enamel pin that could go on the plushie.

To match the vibe, I chose a darker color palette and a special edition of our mascot, Duo. I adapted our master Duo into a more exclusive and... "snobby" version of the owl, achieved by giving him a tuxedo, a streak flame pin, and a less impressed expression.

Documented user flows to visualize user journey

The Streak Society proposal was presented to the entire company. It featured a prompt, an email, a working form to claim a pin, success, and a "not yet" page. On the side, we collaborated with the merch store hackathon team to insert the locked/unlocked Streak Society plush and pin on their presentation as well.

Iteration and launch

A Hackathon hit comes to life as a feature

Executives at Duolingo were excited about the idea they saw at Hackathon and asked us to work on a way to test this initiative at an even lower cost. With a multi-functional strike team of 4, we set out to find a way to launch an MVP. For this, we decided to skip the physical prizes, and went on to decide what Session End screen to present (a Session End screen is a summary screen shown at the end of a lesson or set of challenges that summarizes points and achievements).

We researched what high streak users did, their wants & needs, and what other applications and programs were doing when it came to rewarding loyalty.

Below I'm sharing a bit of the visual story of how we got to the final design. After these and more iterations, we reached our approved version of Streak Society.

Results

A/B Test and Takeaways: it worked!

After running our tests we found that the Streak Society motivates dramatically better streak behavior in users. Duolingo is big on testing and iterating, and we'd run an experiment until we got statistically significant results on key metrics.

We started with a low-cost, unanimated MVP rewarding 365 days instead of 1000, since it would give us results quicker with more users participating. We launched the A/B test on iOS first, and observed results as the experiment ran, as well as after a year.

After a satisfying increase in stats and potential for so much more, our strike team handed this project off to Duolingo's Engagement team with recommendations for next steps, such as unlocking special vanity emojis/icons on the Leaderboard, experimenting with more tiers, exploring exciting animations, and additional rewards. They have fully embraced the feature, and more Streak Society experiments and tweaks are a staple on the team's OKRs every quarter.
